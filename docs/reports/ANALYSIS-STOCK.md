## 1. Recommendation and pilot boundary

KPOne has tested inventory and dispensing building blocks, not an approved production pharmacy system. Keep the existing immutable movement ledger, tenant boundaries, version checks, atomic dispensing completion and saved CA-final billing source. Do not replace these with a second stock engine. Production procurement, receiving, stocktake and adjustment remain parked: `docs/BASELINE.md:163–175` and section 6 item 11 at `docs/BASELINE.md:264`; the repository-wide boundary is in `AGENTS.md` under Non-negotiable boundaries.

Recommended order: establish the unit and physical-stock contract; harden batch eligibility and dispensing selection; make receiving safe; assign ownership of the existing low-stock/expiry lists; add independent variance/write-off controls. Then consider transfers, supplier returns, treatment-room consumption and finance integration. Detailed proposed slices are in section 8. Payroll, accounting and regulatory compliance must not be inferred from stock movements.

The owner's proposed priorities are substantially right, with two qualifications:

- Expiry exclusion and expiry-ordered suggestions already exist. The next work is policy, enforcement, split-batch usability and tests, rather than introducing expiry awareness from zero.
- Passive low-stock and near-expiry indicators already exist. A named daily reviewer and an actionable worklist matter before automated notifications. In contrast, a wrong pack-to-tablet quantity or unverified opening balance can make every subsequent stock figure wrong. Units and opening verification are also pilot-critical.

The clinic's actual ordering, dispensing, checking, storage and counting routines, and any comparison with Yezza, **need owner confirmation**. This report makes no claim about how Yezza works.

Audit ids, versions, operation type, counts and changed field names; do not dump clinical bodies, patient identities, supplier bank details or free-text documents into audit metadata. ## 5. Integration contracts

1. **Doctor order → saved CA final lines → allocation → Complete → ledger → billing/label.** Preserve the doctor's original plan and CA edits as distinct evidence (`docs/DS-01_DISPENSE_EDIT_AND_OTC.md:39–65`). The directory uses `InventoryAvailabilityService`; the inspected `TreatmentPlanService` does not establish a stock reservation. A future consultation availability badge must state it is advisory, branch-local, and rechecked at Complete. It must not promise stock to several patients simultaneously.
2. **Physical stock versus money.** `BillingSourceService.php:75–144` bills final supplied quantities; PO/GR costs do not set selling prices. Refund/reversal does not prove medicine physically returned, accepted, safe to resupply or restored to stock. Separate physical return, quarantine/disposal decision and financial refund with explicit linkage only.
3. **Procurement → AP.** A goods receipt proves recorded quantities, not a supplier invoice, payable, payment or credit. Accounting owns supplier liabilities and bank reconciliation; future exports link immutable receipt/return ids to reviewed supplier bill references. No automatic journal from mutable current balances.
4. **Treatments and consumables.** A performed billable service is not automatically a defined stock recipe. Avoid using fictitious dispensing cases to consume treatment-room materials. Define the operational source before including those quantities in stock reports.
5. **Reports.** Extend the existing movement projection and RPT-01e classification together for any new movement type, including retained history and inactive references. Keep exact per-unit reconciliation and never sum unrelated units. Separate available, held, expired and in-transit quantities from physical closing; a quarantine status change is not necessarily a physical quantity change. Valuation and COGS belong to a separately approved finance contract.

## 7. Policy and regulatory questions reserved for review

No legal requirements, permitted staff activities, register format or statutory retention period are asserted here. The following are all **to confirm with a pharmacist or the regulator**: Poisons Act applicability; controlled/scheduled classifications and registers; pharmacist supervision and permissible delegation; dispensing checks and records; label particulars including batch/expiry; storage and cold-chain monitoring; return-to-stock and disposal rules; recall traceability; required record retention and access.
